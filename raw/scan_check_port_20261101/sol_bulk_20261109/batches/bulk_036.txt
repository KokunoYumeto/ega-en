\subsection{Characterizations of unramified morphisms}
\EGAReaderTarget{ega4.c04.subsec.17-4}{subsection:IV.17.4}

\begin{theorem}[17.4.1]
\label{IV.17.4.1}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation, and $x$ a point of $X$. The following properties are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textup{a)}] $f$ is unramified at the point $x$.
  \item[\textup{b)}] The diagonal morphism $\Delta_f:X\to X\times_Y X$ is a local isomorphism at the point $x$.
  \item[\textup{b$'$)}] If we set $\Delta_f=(\psi,\theta)$, $Z=X\times_Y X$ and $z=\psi(x)$, the homomorphism $\theta_x^\sharp:\sh{O}_{Z,z}\to\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is bijective.
  \item[\textup{b$''$)}] For every morphism $g:Y'\to Y$, and every point $y'\in Y'$ above $y=f(x)$, every $Y'$-section $s'$ of $X'=X\times_Y Y'$ such that $x'=s'(y')$ is above $x$ is a local isomorphism at the point $y'$.
  \item[\textup{c)}] We have $(\varOmega^1_{X/Y})_x=0$.
  \item[\textup{d)}] The $\boldsymbol{k}(y)$-prescheme $f^{-1}(y)$ is unramified over $\boldsymbol{k}(y)$ at the point $x$.
  \item[\textup{d$'$)}] The point $x$ is isolated in $X_y=f^{-1}(y)$ \textup{(in other words (\textbf{Err}$_{\mathrm{III}}$, 20), the morphism $f$ is \emph{quasi-finite at the point} $x$)} and the ring $\sh{O}_{X_y,x}$ is a field which is a separable extension of $\boldsymbol{k}(y)$.
  \item[\textup{d$''$)}] The ring $\sh{O}_{X_y,x}=\sh{O}_{X,x}/\mathfrak{m}_y\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is a field which is a finite separable extension of $\boldsymbol{k}(y)$.
  \item[\textup{e)}] The ring $\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is a formally unramified $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$-algebra for the discrete topologies.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

As $f$ is locally of finite type, the $\sh{O}_X$-Module $\varOmega^1_{X/Y}$ is of finite type (16.3.9), so it amounts to the same to say that $(\varOmega^1_{X/Y})_x=0$ or that there exists an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ such that $\varOmega^1_{X/Y}|U=0$. Taking into account (17.2.1), this proves the equivalence of a) and c). On the other hand, if we set $A=\sh{O}_{Y,y}$, $B=\sh{O}_{X,x}$, we have $(\varOmega^1_{X/Y})_x=\Omega^1_{B/A}$ (16.4.15), and the equivalence of c) and e) therefore follows from (\textbf{0}, 20.7.4).

As d') involves only properties of the morphism $X_y\to\Spec(\boldsymbol{k}(y))$, the equivalence of a) and d') will \emph{ipso facto} imply that of d) and d'). On the other hand, d') and d'') are equivalent, because it amounts to the same to say that $\sh{O}_{X_y,x}$ is a \emph{finite} $\boldsymbol{k}(y)$-algebra or that $x$ is an isolated point of $X_y$, as $X_y$ is a $\boldsymbol{k}(y)$-prescheme locally of finite type (\textbf{I}, 6.4.4).

Let us now prove the equivalence of b) and b'). We may restrict to the case where $Y=\Spec(R)$ and $X=\Spec(S)$ are affine and $f$ is of finite presentation; then we have $Z=\Spec(S\otimes_R S)$ and $\Delta_f$ corresponds to the canonical surjective homomorphism $S\otimes_R S\to S$, whose kernel $\mathfrak{J}$ is, as we know, an ideal of finite type (\textbf{0}, 20.4.4). If we set $\sh{J}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{J}}$, the $\sh{O}_Z$-Module $\psi_*(\sh{O}_X)=\sh{O}_Z/\sh{J}$ is therefore of finite presentation, and the hypothesis that the homomorphism $\theta_z: \sh{O}_{Z,z}\to(\psi_*(\sh{O}_X))_z$ is bijective implies that, on replacing $X$ by an open neighbourhood of $x$ if necessary, the homomorphism $\theta:\sh{O}_Z\to\psi_*(\sh{O}_X)$ is itself bijective ($\mathbf{0}_{\mathrm I}$, 5.2.7). This therefore shows that b') implies b); the converse is evident.

On the other hand, the equivalence of b) and b'') follows from (\textbf{I}, 5.3.7) without any finiteness hypothesis on $f$: indeed, to give a $Y'$-section $s':Y'\to X'$ is equivalent to giving a

$\textit{d'')}\Rightarrow\textit{c)}$: As $\varOmega^1_{X/Y}$ is an $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite type, it follows from Nakayama's lemma that condition \emph{c)} is equivalent to $(\varOmega^1_{X/Y})_x/\mathfrak{m}_y(\varOmega^1_{X/Y})_x=0$, that is (16.4.5), to $(\varOmega^1_{X_y/\Spec(\kres(y))})_x=0$. We are therefore reduced to the case where $Y$ is the spectrum of a field $k$ and $X$ an algebraic $k$-prescheme. The hypothesis that $\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is a field $k'$ which is a finite extension of $k$ implies first that $x$ is \emph{closed} in $X$ (\textbf{I}, 6.4.2), and then that $x$ is a \emph{maximal} point of the Noetherian prescheme $X$, hence is an isolated point of $X$. Replacing $X$ by the open subset $\{x\}$ of $X$, we may therefore suppose that $X=\Spec(k')$; but then the hypothesis that $k'$ is a \emph{finite separable} extension of $k$ implies $\Omega^1_{k'/k}=0$ (\textbf{0}, 20.6.20), which proves c).

$\textit{c)}\Rightarrow\textit{b)}$: We have seen above that we then have $\varOmega^1_{X/Y}|U=0$ for an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ in $X$; assertion \emph{b)} then follows from the definition of $\varOmega^1_{X/Y}$ (16.3.1) and from (16.1.9).

$\textit{b)}\Rightarrow\textit{d'')}$: Replacing $X$ by an open neighbourhood of $x$, we may suppose that $\Delta_f$ is an open immersion; if we denote by $f_y:X_y\to\Spec(\boldsymbol{k}(y))$ the morphism deduced from $f$ by base change, $\Delta_{f_y}$ is then also an open immersion (\textbf{I}, 5.3.4), and as condition \emph{d'')} concerns only the prescheme $X_y$, we see that we may restrict to the case where $Y$ is the spectrum of a field $k$ and $X$ the spectrum of a $k$-algebra $A$ of finite type; property d'') will be established if we prove that $A$ is a \emph{finite and separable} $k$-algebra, such an algebra being a direct product of finite separable extensions of $k$. If $K$ is an algebraically closed extension of $k$, it amounts to the same to say that $A\otimes_k K$ is a finite and separable $K$-algebra (4.6.1), so we see that we may restrict to the case where $k$ is algebraically closed. Let us first show that $A$ is a \emph{finite} $k$-algebra: it will suffice to show that every \emph{closed} point $x$ of $X$ is isolated, for then the set of these points is open in $X$ and discrete, hence finite since it is quasi-compact ($X$ being Noetherian), which will establish our assertion by virtue of (\textbf{I}, 6.4.4). Now, we then have $\boldsymbol{k}(x)=k$ since $k$ is algebraically closed (\textbf{I}, 6.4.3), so there is a $Y$-section $s$ of $X$ such that $s(Y)=\{x\}$, and by virtue of (17.4.1.1), $\{x\}$ is the inverse image of the diagonal $\Delta_X(X)$ under a morphism $X\to X\times_Y X$, hence $\{x\}$ is open in $X$ by virtue of hypothesis b). We have thus shown that $A$ is a

Now, since $A$ is a $k$-algebra, the canonical homomorphism $A\otimes_k A\to A$ can be bijective only if $A=k$. Q.E.D.

\begin{remark}[17.4.1.2]
\label{IV.17.4.1.2}
Suppose only that $f$ is \emph{locally of finite type}. Then $\varOmega^1_{X/Y}$ is again an $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite type (16.3.9), and $\Delta_f$ a morphism locally of finite presentation (1.4.3.1). The whole proof of (17.4.1) is therefore valid, provided that a) is replaced by: \emph{the restriction of $f$ to a suitable neighbourhood of $x$ is a formally unramified morphism}. We see moreover that in this case the restriction of $f$ to a suitable neighbourhood of $x$ is a \emph{locally quasi-finite} morphism.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[17.4.2]
\label{IV.17.4.2}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation. The following properties are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textup{a)}] $f$ is unramified.
  \item[\textup{b)}] The diagonal morphism $\Delta_f:X\to X\times_Y X$ is an open immersion.
  \item[\textup{b$'$)}] For every morphism $Y'\to Y$, every $Y'$-section of $X'=X\times_Y Y'$ is an open immersion.
  \item[\textup{c)}] We have $\varOmega^1_{X/Y}=0$.
  \item[\textup{d)}] For every $y\in Y$, the $\boldsymbol{k}(y)$-prescheme $f^{-1}(y)$ is unramified over $\boldsymbol{k}(y)$.
  \item[\textup{d$'$)}] For every $y\in Y$, the $\boldsymbol{k}(y)$-prescheme $f^{-1}(y)$ is isomorphic to a prescheme of the form $\coprod_{\lambda\in L}\Spec(K_\lambda)$, where, for every $\lambda\in L$, $K_\lambda$ is a finite and separable extension of $\boldsymbol{k}(y)$.
  \item[\textup{e)}] For every $x\in X$, the ring $\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is a formally unramified $\sh{O}_{Y,f(x)}$-algebra for the discrete topologies.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[17.4.3]
\label{IV.17.4.3}
If $f:X\to Y$ is unramified, then $f$ is locally quasi-finite \textup{(\textbf{Err}$_{\mathrm{III}}$, 20)}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[17.4.4]

Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$, and $y=f(x)$. \begin{env}[17.4.5]\label{IV.17.4.5}
\EGAReaderTarget{ega4.c04.statement.17-4-5}{IV.17.4.5}
Given an $S$-prescheme $Y$ and two $S$-morphisms $f:X\to Y$, $g:X\to Y$, we canonically deduce from them an $S$-morphism $(f,g)_S:X\to Y\times_S Y$. We will call the \emph{prescheme of coincidences} of $f$ and $g$ the inverse image under $(f,g)_S$ of the diagonal $\Delta_{Y|S}$; it is therefore a sub-prescheme of $X$, which is \emph{closed} when $Y$ is an $S$-scheme (\textbf{I}, 5.4.1).
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[17.4.6]
\label{IV.17.4.6}
Let $h:Y\to S$ be an unramified morphism, and let $f:X\to Y$, $g:X\to Y$ be two $S$-morphisms. Then the prescheme $C$ of coincidences of $f$ and $g$ is a sub-prescheme induced on an open subset of $X$; if moreover $Y$ is an $S$-scheme \textup{(\textbf{I}, 5.4.1)}, $C$ is a closed sub-prescheme of $X$.
\end{proposition}

Indeed, since $\Delta_h:Y\to Y\times_S Y$ is an open immersion (17.4.2), the inverse image under $(f,g)_S$ of $\Delta_{Y|S}$ is a sub-prescheme induced on an open subset of $X$ (\textbf{I}, 4.4.1). The last assertion follows from (17.4.5).

\begin{corollary}[17.4.7]
\label{IV.17.4.7}
Under the hypotheses of \textup{(17.4.6)}, let $x$ be a point of $X$ such that the two composite morphisms $\Spec(\boldsymbol{k}(x))\to X\xrightarrow{f}Y$ and $\Spec(\boldsymbol{k}(x))\to X\xrightarrow{g}Y$ are equal. Then there exists an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ such that $f|U=g|U$. If moreover $Y$ is an $S$-scheme, there exists an open and closed neighbourhood $X'$ of $x$ in $X$ such that $f|X'=g|X'$. If finally we suppose in addition that $X$ is connected, we have $f=g$.
\end{corollary}

This follows from (17.4.6) and from (\textbf{I}, 5.3.17).

\begin{proposition}[17.5.5]
\label{IV.17.5.5}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$, and $y=f(x)$. Suppose that $Y$ is reduced at the point $y$. Then, for $f$ to be smooth at the point $x$, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ be universally open in a neighbourhood of $x$ in $f^{-1}(y)$ and that $f^{-1}(y)$ be a $\boldsymbol{k}(y)$-prescheme geometrically regular at the point $x$.
\end{proposition}

Taking into account (17.5.1), everything comes down to seeing that, if $f^{-1}(y)$ is a $\boldsymbol{k}(y)$-prescheme geometrically regular at the point $x$, it is equivalent to say that $f$ is flat at the point $x$ or that it is universally open in a neighbourhood of $x$ in $f^{-1}(y)$. Now, if $f$ is flat at the point $x$, it is flat in a neighbourhood of $x$ in $X$ (11.1.1), and consequently it is universally open in this neighbourhood (2.4.6). Conversely, the hypothesis that $f$ is universally open in a neighbourhood of $x$ in $f^{-1}(y)$ and that $f^{-1}(y)$ is a $\boldsymbol{k}(y)$-prescheme geometrically regular at the point $x$ implies that $f$ is flat at the point $x$, since $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ is reduced (15.2.2).

\begin{corollary}[17.5.6]
\label{IV.17.5.6}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$, and $y=f(x)$. Suppose that $Y$ is reduced and geometrically unibranch (6.15.1) at the point $y$. \end{corollary}

Noting that the set of points where $f$ is equidimensional is open (13.3.2), we see that the corollary follows from (17.5.5) and from Chevalley's criterion (14.4.4).

\begin{proposition}[17.7.1]
\label{IV.17.7.1}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation, $g:Y'\to Y$ a morphism, $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, and $f'=f_{(Y')}:X'\to Y'$ and $g':X'\to X$ the canonical projections. Let $x'$ be a point of $X'$, and set $x=g'(x')$, $y'=f'(x')$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textup{(i)}] If $f'$ is unramified at the point $x'$, then $f$ is unramified at the point $x$.
  \item[\textup{(ii)}] Suppose moreover that $g$ is flat at the point $y'$. Then, if $f'$ is smooth \textup{(resp.}\ étale\textup{)} at the point $x'$, $f$ is smooth \textup{(resp.}\ étale\textup{)} at the point $x$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Set $y=f(x)=g(y')$, so that we have $f'^{-1}(y')=f^{-1}(y)\otimes_{\kres(y)}\boldsymbol{k}(y')$; note that $f'$ is locally of finite presentation.

(i) As the property, for a morphism (locally of finite presentation), of being unramified at a point involves only the fibre of the morphism at this point (17.4.1, \emph{d)}), we may restrict to the case where $Y$ and $Y'$ are spectra of fields. But then it amounts to the same to say that $f$ (resp. $f'$) is unramified at $x$ (resp. $x'$) or that it is étale at this point (17.6.1, \emph{c)}), so (i) is a consequence of (ii).

(ii) As $f'$ is flat at the point $x'$ (17.5.1), the hypothesis that $g$ is flat at the point $y'$ implies that $f$ is flat at the point $x$, because the projection $g':X'\to X$ is a morphism flat at the point $x'$, and $f\circ g'=g\circ f'$ is a morphism flat at the point $x'$, whence the conclusion (2.2.11, (iv)). The fact that $f$ is smooth (resp. étale) at the point $x$ then involves only the fibre $f^{-1}(y)$ (17.5.1 and 17.6.1), and we are therefore again reduced to the case where $Y=\Spec(k)$ and $Y'=\Spec(k')$ are spectra of fields. To say that $f'$ is smooth at the point $x'$ then means (17.5.1) that $X'$ is a $k'$-prescheme geometrically regular at the point $x'$, and this implies (6.7.8) that $X$ is a $k$-prescheme geometrically regular at the point $x$, hence that $f$ is smooth at the point $x$. Suppose moreover that $f'$ is étale at the point $x'$, so that $x'$ is isolated in $f'^{-1}(y')$ (17.6.1); as the projection $f'^{-1}(y')\to f^{-1}(y)$ is an open morphism (2.4.10), $x$ is isolated in $f^{-1}(y)$; as we already know that $f$ is smooth at the point $x$, it is étale at this point (17.6.1).

\subsection{Criteria for smoothness and unramifiedness by fibres.}
\EGAReaderTarget{ega4.c04.subsec.17-8}{subsection:IV.17.8}
\label{IV.17.8}

\begin{proposition}[17.8.1]
\label{IV.17.8.1}
Let $g:Y\to S$, $h:X\to S$ be two morphisms locally of finite presentation. For an $S$-morphism $f:X\to Y$ to be unramified, it is necessary and sufficient that for every $s\in S$, the morphism $f_s:h^{-1}(s)\to g^{-1}(s)$ deduced from $f$ by the base change $\Spec(\boldsymbol{k}(s))\to S$ be unramified.
\end{proposition}

This follows immediately from the fact that, for a morphism locally of finite presentation, being unramified is a property of the \emph{fibres} of this morphism (17.4.1, \emph{d)}), and from the fact that, for every $y\in Y$, we have $f^{-1}(y)=f_s^{-1}(y)$ if $s=g(y)$.

\begin{proposition}[17.8.2]
\label{IV.17.8.2}
Let $g:Y\to S$, $h:X\to S$ be two morphisms locally of finite presentation; suppose moreover that $h$ is flat. For an $S$-morphism $f:X\to Y$ to be smooth \textup{(resp.}\ étale\textup{)}, it is necessary and sufficient that, for every $s\in S$, the morphism $f_s:h^{-1}(s)\to g^{-1}(s)$ deduced from $f$ by the base change $\Spec(\boldsymbol{k}(s))\to S$ be smooth \textup{(resp.}\ étale\textup{)}. When this is the case, the morphism $g$ is flat at the points of $f(X)$.
\end{proposition}

Indeed, we know (11.3.10) that for $f$ to be flat, it is necessary and sufficient that $f_s$ be flat for every $s\in S$, and that $g$ is then flat at the points of $f(X)$. But for a flat morphism locally of finite presentation, being smooth is a property of the \emph{fibres} of this morphism (17.5.1, \emph{b)}), and for every $y\in Y$, we have $f^{-1}(y)=f_s^{-1}(y)$ if $s=g(y)$.

\begin{remark}[17.8.3]
\label{IV.17.8.3}
The preceding proofs show (taking into account (11.3.10)) that if the hypotheses on $g$ and $h$ are the same as above, then, for $f$ to be unramified (resp. smooth, resp. étale) \emph{at a point} $x\in X$, it suffices that, if we set $s=h(x)$, $f_s$ be unramified (resp. smooth, resp. étale) at the point $x$.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[17.10.1]\label{IV.17.10.1}
\EGAReaderTarget{ega4.c04.def.17-10-1}{IV.17.10.1}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite type. We call \emph{relative dimension of $f$ at the point $x\in X$} (or \emph{relative dimension of $X$ over $Y$ at the point $x$}), and denote by $\dim_x f$, the nonnegative integer $\dim_x(f^{-1}(f(x)))$.
\end{definition}

To say that $f$ is \emph{quasi-finite} at the point $x$ (\textbf{Err}$_{\mathrm{III}}$, 20) is therefore equivalent to saying that $\dim_x f=0$. We have seen (13.1.3) that the function $x\leadsto\dim_x f$ is upper semi-continuous. Note that, even when the morphism $f$ has property $(S_1)$ (in other words (6.8.1) is flat and such that its fibres have no embedded associated prime cycle), the function $x\leadsto\dim_x f$ is not necessarily continuous, as is shown by the example where $Y=\Spec(k)$, with $k$ a field, and $X=\Spec(k[U,V,W]/\mathfrak p\mathfrak q)$, where $\mathfrak p=(W)$ and $\mathfrak q=(U)+(V\setmin W)$, prime ideals of $k[U,V,W]$ ($X$ thus being the union, in 3-dimensional space, of a plane and a line not parallel to this plane).

To say that a morphism $f:X\to Y$ locally of finite presentation is étale at the point $x\in X$ also means that $f$ is smooth at the point $x$ and that $\dim_x f=0$ (17.6.1).
