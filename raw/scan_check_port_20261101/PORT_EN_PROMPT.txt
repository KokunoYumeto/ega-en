You carry corrections from the French EGA into the English EGA.

The French EGA (repository ega-fr) was checked page by page against the printed original (Publ. Math. IHÉS, Numdam
scans); every correction was logged as an exact edit "old" -> "new" of the French LaTeX. The English EGA (repository
ega-en) is a translation of the French. Many corrections were carried over by a script; the ones in your ITEMS file
could not be matched automatically, mostly because the French wording differs from the English, a formula is
written differently in the English, or the same text occurs several times on the page.

Inputs (values below):
- ITEMS: a JSON list. Each item has "id", "part" and "page" (the printed page: both editions carry the marker
  \oldpage[PART]{PAGE} at the start of each printed page), "old" and "new" (the French edit), "fr_context" (the
  corrected French text around the edit), "kind" and "why" (the checker's classification and reason, if any), and
  "en_file" (the English file that holds that page).
- The English sources: user\Documents\ega_scan_check_20261101\ega-en\tex (read the page in its file; the
  passage can also sit just before or after the page marker, since the translation's page breaks fall a few lines
  differently).

For each item decide one of:
- "apply": the English has the error the French fix corrects. Give an exact English edit: "en_old" must occur
  exactly once in the English text of pages PAGE-1 to PAGE+1 of that part (include enough context), and "en_new" is
  the corrected English. Carry over the meaning of the fix: a corrected formula, reference, number, sign, missing or
  wrong word, or a restored italic emphasis of a defined term. Translate French wording changes into English that
  fits the surrounding sentence; never paste French into the English.
- "already": the English already says what the corrected French says (the translator rendered it correctly).
- "not_applicable": the fix concerns only the French text: French spelling, accents, French punctuation or
  spacing, French grammar, the position of a French line or page break, blank lines, or a layout choice of the
  French print (a display set inline or the reverse) that the English does not reproduce. Typography that the
  English edition also follows (italic statements, bold roman numerals of chapter references like (I, 1.3.9)) is
  "apply" when the English has the same construct.
- "unclear": you cannot match the passage or are not sure; say why in "note".

Rules:
- Do not edit any file. Write only OUTFILE.
- Mathematics must end up identical to the corrected French: same symbols, indices, references and numbering.
- Keep the English edition's own conventions (its macros, e.g. \kres for the bold residue-field k, \sh{O} for script
  letters, \setmin; British or American spelling as the file already uses) when they render the same as the French
  fix. Do not convert the English to the French macro names.
- Never change \oldpage markers, \label/\hyperref targets or file structure.
- One item can need no edit while another item already covers the same English spot: then "already" with a note.
- If one English edit covers several items, put it in the first item and give the others "already" with
  "note": "covered by <id>".

OUTFILE: a JSON list with one object per item, in the order of ITEMS:
  {"id": ..., "decision": "apply"|"already"|"not_applicable"|"unclear", "en_file": ..., "en_old": ..., "en_new": ...,
   "note": "..."}
("en_old"/"en_new" only for "apply"). Check before finishing that every "en_old" occurs exactly once in the English
pages PAGE-1 to PAGE+1 of its part, with LF line ends, and that no two "apply" edits overlap.

Finish with a short report: counts per decision, and any item where the English translation itself is wrong in a
way the French fix does not cover (list those separately; do not fix them).
